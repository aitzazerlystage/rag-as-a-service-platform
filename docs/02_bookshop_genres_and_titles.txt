BOOK SHOP — GENRES WE STOCK & WHAT THEY ARE ABOUT (DOC 2 — FOR RAG / KNOWLEDGE BASE)
================================================================================
This document explains the main types of books we sell, what each genre is
about, and well-known titles customers often ask for. Staff can use it to
recommend sections and to answer “what is this genre?” questions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. LITERARY FICTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is about:
Literary fiction prioritises character, language, and theme over plot alone.
These novels explore human experience—memory, identity, family, morality—and
often experiment with structure or voice. They are chosen for book clubs and
readers who enjoy slow, reflective reads with emotional depth.

Prominent titles we regularly stock or can order:
• To Kill a Mockingbird — Harper Lee (justice, childhood, the American South)
• Beloved — Toni Morrison (trauma, love, and memory after slavery)
• The Remains of the Day — Kazuo Ishiguro (duty, regret, English society)
• A Fine Balance — Rohinton Mistry (survival and dignity in 1970s India)
• The God of Small Things — Arundhati Roy (family, caste, and loss in Kerala)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SCIENCE FICTION & FANTASY (SF/F)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is about:
Science fiction imagines changed worlds—future technology, space societies,
climate futures—and asks “what if?” Fantasy builds secondary worlds with magic,
myth, and quests. Both genres explore power, freedom, and what makes us human,
often at epic scale. We shelve SF and fantasy together with clear spine labels.

Prominent titles we regularly stock or can order:
• Dune — Frank Herbert (ecology, empire, and prophecy on a desert planet)
• The Lord of the Rings — J.R.R. Tolkien (classic high fantasy quest)
• The Left Hand of Darkness — Ursula K. Le Guin (gender, diplomacy, ice world)
• Neuromancer — William Gibson (cyberpunk, AI, and corporate power)
• The Name of the Wind — Patrick Rothfuss (magic school, legend, and music)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MYSTERY, CRIME & THRILLER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is about:
Mystery and crime fiction centre on puzzles, investigation, and moral grey
zones—who did it, why, and whether justice is served. Thrillers add pace and
danger: ticking clocks, conspiracies, and high stakes. Ideal for readers who
want tension, twists, and strong hooks.

Prominent titles we regularly stock or can order:
• Murder on the Orient Express — Agatha Christie (classic locked-room mystery)
• The Big Sleep — Raymond Chandler (hardboiled Los Angeles PI)
• The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo — Stieg Larsson (Nordic crime, conspiracy)
• Gone Girl — Gillian Flynn (psychological thriller, unreliable narrators)
• The No. 1 Ladies’ Detective Agency — Alexander McCall Smith (gentle Botswana mysteries)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HISTORY, POLITICS & BIOGRAPHY (NON-FICTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is about:
These books explain real events, movements, and lives: how societies formed,
wars and peace, leaders and ordinary people, and ideas that shaped the world.
Biography and memoir focus on one life’s arc; political and historical works
connect past to present. We group narrative history with political analysis and
life writing in adjacent bays.

Prominent titles we regularly stock or can order:
• Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind — Yuval Noah Harari (big-picture human history)
• The Diary of a Young Girl — Anne Frank (WWII, hope, and resilience)
• Team of Rivals — Doris Kearns Goodwin (Lincoln, cabinet, American Civil War era)
• Long Walk to Freedom — Nelson Mandela (struggle, prison, presidency)
• The Silk Roads — Peter Frankopan (global history through Central Asia and trade)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CHILDREN’S & MIDDLE GRADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is about:
Picture books and early readers build language and joy; middle grade (roughly
ages 8–12) offers adventure, friendship, and identity with age-appropriate
themes. We emphasise diverse voices, humour, and gateway series that turn young
readers into lifelong book buyers.

Prominent titles we regularly stock or can order:
• Charlotte’s Web — E.B. White (friendship on a farm; classic middle grade)
• Matilda — Roald Dahl (clever child, kindness vs cruelty)
• Percy Jackson and the Lightning Thief — Rick Riordan (Greek myths, modern adventure)
• The Tale of Peter Rabbit — Beatrix Potter (beloved illustrated classic)
• Wonder — R.J. Palacio (kindness, school, looking different)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SCIENCE, NATURE & POPULAR SCIENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is about:
Popular science translates research into clear stories: physics, biology,
ecology, medicine, and space. Nature writing combines observation with memoir
and conservation. These books suit curious general readers and students who want
big ideas without a textbook tone.

Prominent titles we regularly stock or can order:
• A Brief History of Time — Stephen Hawking (cosmology, black holes, time)
• Silent Spring — Rachel Carson (environment, pesticides, modern ecology)
• The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks — Rebecca Skloot (ethics, cells, family)
• Cosmos — Carl Sagan (universe, science as a human story; companion to the series)
• The Sixth Extinction — Elizabeth Kolbert (mass extinction, climate, reporting)
